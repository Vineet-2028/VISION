## F. Async multi-rate architecture

Only the main pipeline mutates tracker/world/reasoning state. Immutable
enrichment jobs are submitted before detection and completed results polled
afterward without a barrier. Source identity, session, timestamps and freshness
are checked; old depth/flow cannot be sampled against newer object boxes.
Shutdown joins workers and surfaces previously unobserved failures.

## AE. Root-cause ranking

Original: acquisition/catch-up conversion, then synchronous depth, then other
perception, then domain processing. After rescue, detector and CPU optional-worker
contention dominate more than bounded queue operations. Final per-stage quantiles
and first-crossing counts support this ranking, not exact causal percentage claims.

## AF. Primary bottleneck

Fresh perception/acquisition cadence under host load. The all-provider run proves
that CPU depth can consume resources while every result is unusably old; final
selection removes that provider explicitly. GPU model runtime is verified, not assumed.

## AG. Secondary bottleneck

Full-state tracking, trajectory and interaction remain measurable. Final six-sibling
reasoning-span sum P95 43.984 ms, versus 56.769 ms in
realtime-04. Dense states and host contention differ from the 16-snapshot benchmark.
Optional semantic surface processing also remains expensive.

## AH. Components not established as primary bottlenecks

Risk/attention/alert stages and bounded scheduling are not the original dominant
cost. Pure reasoning on the original 16-snapshot/20-pass protocol: canonical P95
7.579 ms, initial rescue rerun 20.950 ms, geometry-optimized 6.730 ms. Bounded
immutable edge preparation removes repeated work; decisions are never cached.
This microbenchmark cannot establish uniformly single-digit full-state latency.
